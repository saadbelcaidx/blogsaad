It appears to me that this week I am going back to a hotel I once knew when I was dead broke.

In those days, I would sometimes find a way into beautiful places before I had built a beautiful life. I wanted to be near the polished walls, the gardens, the beautiful rooms, the quiet water. Sometimes I went with a beautiful woman, and perhaps part of the pleasure was that, for a few days, I could step into a life that did not yet belong to me. I had no money, no structure, no real authority over my circumstances. I had my body, my charm, and an appetite for life that exceeded the life I could afford.

Now I return to the same place from another position.

The walls may be unchanged. The corridors may carry the same light. But I am not the same man walking through them. I no longer have to borrow the atmosphere. I can pay for every room, continue my work from there, rest, and leave without the place swallowing my entire world. What used to feel like a performance is now just an ordinary choice.

I think this is one of my gifts, though it may also be one of my torments.

I notice relationships between things.

I notice the small change in someone's voice before I know what it means. I notice when warmth withdraws, when respect shifts, when a sentence carries more than its words. A room is rarely only a room to me. A wall can hold another year. A familiar hallway can suddenly open, and behind it stands the man I was when I first walked there.

This same thing follows me into work.

I look at one company and see that it is already spending money to reach another. I look at the second and see that it already has a live need. I notice the event, the budget, the timing, the person who must decide, and the supplier already hunting for the opportunity. I am rarely looking at two companies. I am looking at the line between them.

It is the same faculty pointed in two directions. Inward it looks for meaning, outward it looks for opportunity, and most days I cannot tell where one ends and the other begins.

I do not think the gift is really about recognizing patterns. I feel the relationship between things before it has a name.

But this is also where the torment enters. The same mind that finds an opportunity also catches every hesitation. The eye that finds the hidden commercial bridge can search endlessly for hidden meaning in a glance, a silence, a delay, a wall. It makes the world feel alive with meaning. It also makes it hard for anything to feel innocent.

I do not think the answer is to see less. I think it is to know when I am reading poetry into something, when I am reading work into it, and when I am only afraid.

This week I will return to the hotel. I expect the old images to rise. I expect the walls to remember the young man who came there with no money, only charm, hunger, and a body he had disciplined into beauty.

But I am not going back to become him again. I am going back as the one who kept his hunger and gave it some structure.

He could see the life before he had it. I can walk into it now without pretending.

And perhaps that is what the return is for, to let the two men finally recognize one another across the same room, love you both.
