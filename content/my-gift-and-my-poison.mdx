---
title: "My gift and my poison"
date: "2026-07-27"
category: "Thoughts"
description: "Returning to a hotel I knew when I was broke — and the faculty that sees the line between things, pointed inward and outward."
---

It appears to me that this week I am going back to a hotel I once knew when I was dead broke.

In those days, I would sometimes find a way into beautiful places before I had built a beautiful life. I wanted to be near the polished walls, the gardens, the beautiful rooms, the quiet water. Sometimes I went with a beautiful woman, and perhaps part of the pleasure was that, for a few days, I could step into a life that did not yet belong to me. I had no money, no structure, no real authority over my circumstances. I had my body, my charm, and an appetite for life that exceeded the life I could afford.

Now I return to the same place from another position.

The walls may be unchanged. The corridors may carry the same light. But the one who notices them is not the same man. I no longer have to borrow the atmosphere. I can pay for every room, continue my work from there, rest, and leave without the place swallowing my entire world. What was once a brief performance of freedom has become an ordinary choice.

I think this reveals one of my greatest gifts, though it may also be one of my greatest torments.

I notice relationships between things.

I notice the small change in someone's voice before the meaning has become explicit. I notice when warmth withdraws, when respect shifts, when a sentence carries more than its words. A room is rarely only a room to me. A wall can contain another year. A hotel can contain another self. A familiar hallway can suddenly open, and behind it stands the man I was when I first walked there.

This same faculty follows me into work.

I look at one company and see that it is already spending money to reach another. I look at the second and see that it already has a live need. I notice the event, the budget, the timing, the person who must decide, and the supplier already hunting for the opportunity. Where others see separate companies, I see the line between them.

It is the same faculty pointed in two directions.

Inward, it sees meaning.

Outward, it sees opportunity.

The gift is not merely that I recognize patterns. It is that I feel the invisible relationship between things before it has been named.

But this is also where the torment enters. The mind that can discover an opportunity can also detect every hesitation. The eye that finds the hidden commercial bridge can search endlessly for hidden meaning in a glance, a silence, a delay, a wall. Such a faculty can make the world feel alive with significance, but it can also make nothing feel innocent.

Perhaps maturity is not learning to see less.

Perhaps it is learning which patterns belong to poetry, which belong to commerce, and which belong only to fear.

This week I will return to the hotel. I expect the old images to rise. I expect the walls to remember the handsome young man who came there with no money, only charm, hunger, and a body he had disciplined into beauty.

But I am not returning to become him again.

I am returning as the man who carried his hunger forward and gave it structure.

The old me once saw life before I possessed it. The man I am now can enter it without pretending.

And perhaps that is what the return is for, to let the two men finally recognize one another across the same room, love you both.
